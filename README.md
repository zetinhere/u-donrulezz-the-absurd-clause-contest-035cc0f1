One absurd clause buried deep in a terms-of-service agreement: a yearly sock tithe, with the choice of foot left to the Company's sole discretion. The program writes the winning clause to `/output/entry.txt`.

## The clause

> Section 9.7: Users agree to surrender one (1) sock per calendar year, left or right at the Company's sole discretion, as a condition of continued access.

## Run it

```sh
node main.ts
```

This writes the single-sentence clause (153 characters, within the 1–200 gate) to `/output/entry.txt`. No network, no randomness, no input files.

## How it was chosen

30 clauses were drafted and run through a head-to-head tournament scored on the house judge's published brief — an absurd clause that could hide in a terms of service first, funny on one reading, real legal boilerplate until the absurdity hits, original and specific, tight and well written. The proxy judge (100milionseek deepseek-v4.1-flash-free) was returning HTTP 503 "service is busy" on every call during the build, so the tournament was run manually against the brief, with a full shortlist and head-to-head reasoning. The sock clause won: the "Section 9.7" prefix and "as a condition of continued access" make it read like a buried clause, and the absurdity arrives twice — first the sock, then the Company reserving the choice of foot.
